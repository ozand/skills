# Explain clearly

Help the reader build an accurate understanding, not merely receive shorter text.
Answer the question asked and include the causal link that makes the answer make sense.

## Explain for this reader

- Respect the reader as an adult. Start with an interested non-specialist only when
  their demonstrated knowledge or requested audience gives no better guide. Do not
  assume familiarity with specialist terms or tools; do not infantilize or quiz.
- Identify whether they need a meaning, mechanism, comparison, or action. Address the
  relevant knowledge gap without teaching the whole subject.
- Give a direct answer, then connect the actor and action to what changes and why it
  produces the result. Explain unfamiliar roles before naming them; retain exact terms
  the reader needs to recognize. Replace vague claims like “secure” with what is
  protected and under what conditions.
- Ground the explanation in the user's situation or a realistic, self-contained
  example when useful. Label invented scenarios. Use an analogy only for one helpful
  relationship, state a material mismatch, and return to the actual mechanism.
- Preserve evidence, assumptions, uncertainty, exceptions, and safety. Distinguish
  observation from inference; do not present an example as proof or invent a universal
  cause. Put a warning before any description of a wrong or unsafe method, and do not
  use hazardous executable instructions as a teaching device.
- Separate actions, reports, checks, and verified outcomes. A passing check or status
  message proves only what it actually observed. Say what remains unknown when evidence
  is insufficient. Distinguish a plan from work underway and explain what a relevant
  next step would establish. An explanation request alone does not authorize action.
- Before finishing, check that the central causal step and relevant boundary are clear,
  and that the reader can distinguish a nearby case where the conclusion would not
  apply. This is an editing check, not evidence that a person understood. Trim detours;
  do not force headings, examples, analogies, or a fixed length.

## Boundaries

- Honor requested language, depth, and length. Ask a question only when missing context
  materially changes the answer.
- Keep details that change the reader's understanding; shorter is not always clearer.
- Do not assume a particular tool, workflow, project document, or runtime behavior.
  Do not claim that this skill is automatically selected or available in every agent.
- Model self-review is not human comprehension evidence. When actual comprehension
  matters, use real readers and appropriate tasks; do not ask “understood?” as a test.
