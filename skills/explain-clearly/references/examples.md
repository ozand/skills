# Examples

These fictional rewrites illustrate editorial choices; they are not reports of real
incidents, verified diagnoses, or evidence that readers understood.

## Everyday, nontechnical: why a plant wilts

Before: “The plant is stressed from insufficient water.”

After: “If the soil has dried out, the roots cannot take up enough water to replace what
the leaves lose, so the leaves may droop. Check the soil before watering: drooping can
have other causes, and adding water to already-wet soil may make matters worse.”

The example connects a possible cause to a visible result and avoids treating one symptom
as proof of a diagnosis.

## Technical: why a browser shows an old picture

Before: “The browser cache served a stale asset.”

After: “Your browser may have saved an earlier copy of the picture and reused it instead
of downloading the replacement. Those saved files are called the browser cache. Clearing
them can make the browser download the picture again, but it will not fix an image that
is wrong on the site itself.”

The mechanism is concrete, the term follows its meaning, and the proposed remedy is
limited to one possible cause.

## Privacy: what a location permission permits

Before: “The app has access to your location.”

After: “This permission lets the app request your device's location in the situations
shown in the permission settings. It does not, by itself, tell us when the app actually
requested it, what it stored, or who else received it; those depend on the app's
behavior and the device's controls.”

The explanation separates permission from observed use and data handling. The exact
scope depends on the operating system and settings; this illustration is not a claim
about a particular app.
